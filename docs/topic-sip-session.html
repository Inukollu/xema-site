sip-session

A SIP session is the full list of SIP messages for one call, in order. Each block is one message, numbered, with its time and direction. The messages are shown exactly as captured.

Colours

  • Green on a number is the caller ID as it is presented. It is marked wherever the call carries it: in From, and in the identity your carrier asserts for the caller, so any difference between them is visible.
  • Red is a rejection: any response from 400 upwards, with its reason.
  • Amber is a provisional response: trying, ringing or redirected.
  • Green on a response means the call was answered.
  • Header names are dimmed so the values are easier to read; important headers keep their colour. The m= line of the media offer is highlighted, because codec rejections relate to that line.

Call sequence

  • An INVITE starts the call, with the caller, the dialled number and the audio formats the caller supports.
  • Responses below 200, such as 100 Trying and 180 Ringing, are provisional.
  • One final response decides the outcome: 200 OK means the call is answered; 400 or above means it is rejected.
  • An ACK confirms the final response. A BYE and its 200 OK end an answered call.

Common rejections

  • 401 / 407: authentication is required. This is normal; the caller usually retries at once, and the retry is listed separately.
  • 403 Forbidden: recognised but not allowed. Usually the source address is not accepted, or the credentials are wrong.
  • 404 Not Found: no number is configured for the dialled number. Check the dialled number against your configured numbers.
  • 408 / 480 / 503: no answer in time, nobody available, or the remote end is not accepting calls.
  • 486 Busy: the destination is busy.
  • 488 Not Acceptable Here: the two ends could not agree on an audio format. Compare the m= line of the offer with the answer. If the caller retries with a different offer, the difference shows the cause.

Sharing

  • Carriers usually ask for the whole exchange when you report a problem with their calls.
  • The exchange contains callers' numbers and addresses, so handle it as you would any call record.
